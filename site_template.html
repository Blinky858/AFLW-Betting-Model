AFLW Goalkicker Model

How the model works

Expected goals = team's expected shots × player's share of shots × player's accuracy.

Team shots come from the team's recent shots for, the opponent's recent shots conceded, and home ground. Share is the player's recent share of her team's shots, pulled toward the average for the position she's named in. The 21 named players' shares add up to 100%, so a missing forward's shots go to whoever replaces her. Accuracy is goals ÷ shots, pulled toward the league rate of about 41%. Goals follow a negative binomial distribution, which gives the 1+, 2+ and 3+ probabilities.

Data comes from the AFL's official stats feed. The site rebuilds itself automatically every few hours: results update after each round, and lineups update once clubs name their teams (usually Thursday).

Using it

Enter a bookie's price next to a player. Edge = model probability × odds − 1. Only bets with an edge of 10% or more are flagged, because the model has error and the bookie may know something it doesn't (late outs, role changes). The suggested stake is a quarter of what the Kelly formula recommends, capped at 2% of your bank.

Before teams are named, the odds use each side's last lineup. Check that the match card shows "Teams named" before you bet.

This model hasn't yet shown an edge against bookies. It beats simple methods, but beating the bookie is the real test. Log the odds you take and the closing odds. Consistently beating the closing price is the evidence to look for.
